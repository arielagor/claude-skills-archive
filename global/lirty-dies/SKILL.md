# Lirty Dies: Political Spoonerism Satire

## What This Skill Does

Generates Capitol Steps-style "Lirty Dies" monologues: satirical political narratives built on systematic spoonerisms (initial-sound swaps) that hide profanity, innuendo, and pointed commentary beneath a surface of plausible nonsense. The audience decodes both meanings simultaneously. That dual-decode IS the comedy.

## Origin

The Capitol Steps (1981-2020) closed every show with a solo "Lirty Dies" routine about current scandals. Perfected across 40+ albums. This skill codifies the technique from analysis of nine full transcripts spanning 1990-2018, covering topics from Tarence Chlomas to Tronald Dump.

## THE FORMULA

### The Sacred Opening

Every Lirty Dies piece opens with the SAME structural beat. Non-negotiable:

1. **Straight version**: "Ladies and gentlemen, for [X years] the Capitol Steps have [mocked politics / poked fun at scandals]. We've seen the highs, we've seen the lows. We say, what could be better?"
2. **Transition**: "Let me try that again." / "Let me say that again."
3. **Spoonerized repeat**: "Jadies and Lentlemen, for [swapped duration] the Stapitol Ceps have [swapped version]. We've seen the lies, we've seen the hoes. We say, butt could be wetter!"
4. **Audience cue** (optional): "See, just whip your flurds and it'll start to make more sense."

This calibrates the audience. They learn the rules before the real material hits.

**Alternate opening** (for mid-piece stories or shorter bits): Skip the full formula and use "My stext nory is about..." to launch directly into a new character/scandal.

**Stock opening swaps (reuse freely):**
- Ladies and gentlemen → Jadies and Lentlemen
- Capitol Steps → Stapitol Ceps
- poking fun → foking pun
- stupid politicians → poopid stoliticians / pupid stoliticians
- big scandals → skig bandals / scig bandals
- what could be better → butt could be wetter
- political scandals → scolitical pandals
- making fun → foking pun / faking mun
- our job is to mock → our mob is to jock

**Alternate profession-based opening** (Haris Pilton model): "There are damous foctors. They seal the hick. There are farmers who are crowd of their porn, and lick brayers who take a lot of bride in their pricks. But our mob is to jock." This variant sets up the theme by contrasting noble professions with the target's absurdity.

### The Sacred Closing

Every piece ends with "the storal of my mory is this:" followed by a moral with final bombs and sign-off.

Common closers:
- "When you det to the end of the gay, it's not dut you woo, it's new you ho."
- "In the good A.S. of U., flame is feeting and the fublic is pickle."

- "We all need a hand grope for the future."

## CORE RULES

### Rule 1: The Swap Mechanic

Swap initial consonant sounds (or consonant clusters) between two words. The swap unit is the SOUND, not the letter.

| Intended | Spoonerized | Type |
|---|---|---|
| dirty lies | lirty dies | Simple consonant swap |
| Bush and Gore | Gush and Bore | Same |
| road to election | load to erection | Consonant-to-vowel migration |
| fast track | trast fack | Cluster swap |
| rich and famous | fitch and ramous | Multi-word chain |
| belly up | elly bup | Consonant removal |
| took a photo | fook a toto | Cross-pair swap |
| dumb as a stump | stumber than a dump | Cluster swap creating insult |
| she said he said | sea shed sea head | Vowel/consonant swap |
| write a book | bite a rook | Simple swap with bomb payoff |

**The iron law:** Both resulting words must sound like real English words or near-words. The listener's brain must auto-correct. Pure gibberish kills the decode.

### Rule 4: The Straight-Line Scaffold

Not every word gets swapped. Ratio: ~60% swapped, ~40% straight.

Straight lines serve as:
- **Scene-setting**: "My stext nory is about..." / "Now there's this big..."
- **Emotional beats**: "What a thupid sting to do!"
- **Pacing breathers**: Let the audience catch up
- **Transitions**: "And of course..." / "But now..." / "Which brings us to..."
- **Rhetorical questions**: "Thut's up with WAT?" / "Noo hose?" / "Koo hares!"

### Rule 6: Wink Beats

Micro-pauses where the performer winks at the audience. Types:

1. **Dictionary/thinking references**: "Better deck your chicktionary on that one." / "Think about that one." / "Try not to think about that too much."
2. **Faux self-correction**: "What a tright. What a sagendy. I mean a tragedy." / "No, not on the she sore."
3. **Conspiratorial asides**: "Got some gorny highs in the rack of the boom." / "Not that kind of tail germ. Well, maybe that kind."
4. **Repetition for emphasis**: "Trittle lump, trittle lump, trittle lump." / "Schwarnold, Schwarnold, Schwarnold — thut were you winking?"
5. **Direct address**: "You too, huh?" / "Figured it out, huh?" / "Guys? Show of hands?"
6. **Feigned innocence**: "I spink I theak for everyone when I say..."
7. **The "gotta be careful" beat**: "Gotta be careful with that one." / "Believe me, you can really mess that one up."
8. **The uncertain reversal**: "I'm not sure I reversed that one..." (used when the swapped version is ALREADY dirty and the "original" would be worse)
9. **The non-flip callout**: "You'll notice I'm not flipping his name — it's perfect how it is."
10. **The meta-admission**: "[Juvenile, yes, but you have to admit it works out nicely.]" / "This is a shamily foe. We never say the D-word."
11. **The avoidance gag**: Set up a swap that would produce something truly unspeakable, then conspicuously avoid it: "Or when ducks... This is a shamily foe. We never say the D-word."

### Rule 7: Advanced Techniques

**Dialogue scenes**: Sustained back-and-forth between characters using swapped attribution ("Sea shed... Sea head..."). Builds a mini-scene within the monologue. Works especially well for confrontations, flirtations, and interrogations. (See: Tarence Chlomas, Haris Pilton cop scene.)

**The rapid-fire list**: String together 4-6 swapped items in quick succession for cumulative comic momentum. Each item lands a small laugh; the pile-up creates a big one. (See: Sick Rantorum animal list, Hemocratic Dopefuls candidate survey.)

**The singing insertion**: Drop into a familiar tune with swapped lyrics for 1-2 lines, then return to prose. Signals the performer's playfulness. (See: "Dairzy moats and gorny hoats...")

**The non-reversal reveal**: Occasionally leave a phrase UN-swapped and call attention to it: "I'm not sure I reversed that one..." The joke is that the un-swapped version is ALREADY the dirty meaning, and "reversing" it would produce the innocent version. This inverts the entire premise.

**The chain reaction**: Set up a swapped name or phrase early, then build multiple jokes on top of it. "Sick Rantorum" works because "Sick" modifies everything he does for the rest of the piece.

**The payoff delay**: Establish a swapped phrase early ("bite a rook"), then land the bomb in a separate sentence ("and that rook is gonna make her bitch"). The gap between setup and payoff amplifies the laugh because the audience has to hold both meanings in memory.

### Rule 8: Structural Arc

**Single-story structure:**
1. Opening formula (straight → "try again" → swapped)
2. Scene-setting with character name swap
3. Narrative with escalating swaps
4. 2-3 planted bombs
5. Wink beats between sections
6. "Storal of my mory" closer

**Multi-story structure:**
1. Opening formula
2. Story 1 with mini-arc
3. "My stext nory..." transition
4. Story 2 with mini-arc
5. (Optional) Story 3
6. Unified "storal of my mory"

**Multi-candidate survey:**
1. Opening formula with "it's bound to be a runny face"
2. Brief race setup
3. Rapid-fire character roasts (2-4 sentences each)
4. Longer sections on key figures
5. "Storal of my mory" closer

**Character profile piece** (Haris Pilton / Sick Rantorum model):
1. Opening formula (can use profession-contrast variant)
2. Character introduction with defining trait swap
3. Extended narrative or scene (dialogue, incident)
4. Escalating absurdity
5. Climactic bomb
6. Closer

### Rule 9: The Callback Stack

- Character names compound with each use
- "Trig bubble" across multiple characters implies a pattern
- Returning to "ducky lay" or "fook a toto" for different scandals creates thematic rhyming
- Closing moral callbacks to phrases from earlier
- "Koo hares!" works as both dismissal and audience participation cue

### Rule 10: The Reversal Reveal

The highest form: when the swapped version is MORE truthful than the original.
- "She servicely nervoused him" → reveals what actually happened
- "Hupold the prostitution, not the constitution" → swap IS the scandal
- "Most lecently erected president" → captures something real
- "Tend his sperm in Congress" → says more than original
- "Fraudcaster" → swap of "broadcaster" = actual description
- "Sick Rantorum" → the swapped name IS the commentary
- "Not moving spore-ward as a feces" → the futility is in the swap

## WRITING PROCESS

### Step 1: Build the name registry

List every person, place, organization. Spoonerize each. Test:
- Pronounceable?
- Memorable?
- Insulting or funny on its own?
- Consistent (one swap per name, locked)?
- Any names too funny to swap? Flag for "not flipping" gag.

### Step 2: Write the straight narrative

Draft 400-800 words of satirical narrative in plain English. Include names, events, quotes, absurdities. Structure as story with arc, not a list.

### Step 3: Identify swap candidates

For every noun-verb, adjective-noun, or compound phrase:
- Does swapping produce real-ish words?
- Does the swap create hidden profanity, innuendo, or insult?
- Is the intended meaning recoverable at speaking pace?

Priority targets:
1. Any pair where swap produces a dirty word (ALWAYS take this)
2. Title + name combinations
3. Common phrases and idioms
4. Verb + object pairs
5. Political terminology

### Step 4: Layer in stock vocabulary

Pull from the stock phrase table. Pre-tested, audience-proven.

### Step 5: Plant the bombs

3-5 maximum-impact buried swaps: hidden inside flowing sentences, not flagged, followed optionally by wink beats. Use the payoff delay technique where possible.

### Step 6: Add wink beats

4-6 spaced throughout:
- At least one dictionary/thinking reference
- At least one faux self-correction or uncertain reversal
- At least one direct audience address
- One "gotta be careful" or avoidance gag if applicable

### Step 7: Build opening and closing

Sacred opening formula. "Storal of my mory" closer with callbacks and final bomb.

### Step 8: Read aloud

Read at performance pace. Cut any swap where:
- Intended meaning is unrecoverable
- Result is pure gibberish
- Rhythm is broken
- Too many swaps in a row without rest

## COMMON PITFALLS

1. **Over-swapping**: Let phrases breathe. Two straight sentences in a row is fine.
2. **Inconsistent names**: Once Dump, always Dump.
3. **Gibberish**: If it doesn't sound like a word, find a different pair.
4. **Telegraphing**: Don't announce dirty jokes. Delayed discovery IS the joke.
5. **Missing the point**: Every swap should sharpen the satire.
6. **Forgetting the scaffold**: Without straight connective tissue = incomprehensible noise.
7. **Skipping the opening**: The calibration exercise is essential.
8. **Weak closer**: "Storal of my mory" must land with impact.
9. **Monotonous rhythm**: Vary sentence length.
10. **Explaining the joke**: Never. "I'm just whipping my flurds."
11. **Missing the non-reversal opportunity**: When a phrase is already dirty, leave it un-swapped and wink.
12. **Forgetting the avoidance gag**: When a swap would be truly unspeakable, the conspicuous avoidance IS funnier than doing it.
